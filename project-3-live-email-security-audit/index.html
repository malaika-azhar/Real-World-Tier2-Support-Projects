Key finding: Authentication and spam-filtering are two separate systems. Splunk's email passed SPF/DKIM/DMARC cleanly but was still routed to spam on content grounds — passing auth confirms sender identity, not that the mail is wanted or safe. This is the exact distinction a SOC analyst has to make when triaging inbox alerts.

Why no FAIL example: All 4 sampled headers passed. Modern providers enforce authentication aggressively enough that outright failures rarely reach an inbox or spam folder — they're bounced or silently rejected earlier. A 4/4 PASS sample is evidence the enforcement layer is working, not a gap in the audit.

What I learned & limitations

Docs

What I learned: How to read and compare SPF/DMARC at the DNS level across real organizations, the practical gap between soft-fail (~all) and hard-fail (-all) SPF, how DMARC policy tiers translate into real enforcement, and that a passing authentication check does not mean an email is safe or wanted.

✕
DKIM not enumerated per domain→ Selectors are arbitrary and not discoverable via blind DNS lookup — out of scope for a passive audit
✕
No FAIL example in the header sample→ Gmail's inbound filtering is strict enough that failed-auth mail rarely survives to inbox — noted as a finding, not a gap
✕
Small-business sample size is 2→ Enough to show a pattern, too small to generalize — treated as indicative, not exhaustive
